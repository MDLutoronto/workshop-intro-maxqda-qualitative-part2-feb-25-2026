# Workshop Recording: Introduction to Using MAXQDA for Qualitative Data Analysis: Part 2 (Feb. 25, 2026)

This page provides a video recording of a Map and Data Library MAXQDA workshop.

**Workshop Description:**

**What:** Building upon the [Introduction to Using MAXQDA for Qualitative Data Analysis: Part 1](https://mdlutoronto.github.io/workshop-intro-maxqda-qualitative-part1-feb-24-2026/) workshop, learn to use some of MAXQDA’s analysis and visualization tools, and how to work with document variables.

**Why:** Learning qualitative data analysis skills and tools will help you organize and participate in qualitative research projects, gain insights into your data, communicate your findings, and stand out in the job market. If you want to explore an excellent alternative to [NVivo](https://lumivero.com/products/nvivo/), you will want to check this tool out!

**Software:** Faculty, staff, and students at the University of Toronto can now get a free license key for MAXQDA. Check out our [installation guide](https://mdlutoronto.github.io/MAXQDA-installation-license/) for instructions. You can also use it in the [Robarts Library](https://library.utoronto.ca/library/robarts) 4th floor [computer lab](https://library.utoronto.ca/space/robarts-library/robarts-library-electronic-classroom) and the Map & Data Library (MDL) [data lab](https://library.utoronto.ca/space/mdl/map-data-librarys-data-lab) on the 5th floor.

**Who:** This workshop is designed for those new to MAXQDA’s analysis and visualization tools, and its document variables features, but assumes basic knowledge of using MAXQDA to load in data and work with codes. This prior knowledge can be obtained by first taking the [Introduction to Using MAXQDA for Qualitative Data Analysis: Part 1](https://mdlutoronto.github.io/workshop-intro-maxqda-qualitative-part1-feb-24-2026/) workshop. Skills gained in this workshop can help participants working with qualitative data from any discipline: Humanities, Social Sciences, or Sciences. There are no prerequisites or assumptions of knowledge of statistics or programming.

**How:** This is a 1.5-hour in-person workshop (but a shorter recording time due to activity breaks), consisting of a short lecture and then a series of demonstrations, which you can follow along with or just watch, interspersed with self-paced activities.
